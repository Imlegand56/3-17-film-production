# Treatment — 3:17

## Title
3:17

## Genre
Crime Mystery Thriller

## Logline
When a mysterious emergency call from Adam Mansour’s phone ends with a whisper of warning, Detective Samer is drawn into a locked-room murder scene where the victim’s note reads: “I KNOW WHO DID IT.” As the investigation deepens, Samer uncovers a web of financial conspiracy, hidden identities, secret recordings, and a brother who may have returned from the dead—until the final revelation exposes that nothing in this case is what it first appeared to be.

## Premise

In a forgotten Egyptian apartment building, a call is made from Adam Mansour’s phone at 3:17 AM. It contains only heavy breathing, a metallic sound, and a chilling whisper: “Don’t look behind you.” Moments later, Adam is found dead in his apartment. The front door is locked from the inside. There are no signs of forced entry. A wall clock has stopped at exactly 3:17. On the desk lies a single note: “I KNOW WHO DID IT.”

Detective Samer is assigned to the case. What seems at first like an impossible homicide quickly transforms into a complex investigation involving a missing brother, a close friend with suspicious ties, a hidden financial trail, and a hidden set of recordings Adam left behind.

The deeper Samer digs, the more he realizes that Adam’s death may be the result of a meticulously staged disappearance. Adam had not just discovered something dangerous—he had been preparing to expose it.

## Story Structure

### Act I – The Locked Room

At 3:31 AM, officers arrive at Adam’s apartment. The door is locked from inside. The apartment is dark, silent, and unnaturally still. The television is running without sound. There is a coffee cup on the table. Adam lies dead on the floor.

Detective Samer notices immediately that the old wall clock has stopped at 3:17. He also notices the note: “I KNOW WHO DID IT.” He begins to suspect that the scene has been staged or manipulated. The apartment contains no obvious signs of any intruder. The body appears to be Adam’s, but the timeline is impossible.

The police recover the 11-second emergency call, which offers only breathing and a metallic sound. A whisper at the end is heard: “Don’t look behind you.” No one knows who spoke. Samer begins to ask why Adam would call the police if he had already been killed.

### Act II – The Suspect and the Secrets

Adam’s close friend Ramy becomes the first major suspect. He had spoken to Adam shortly before the murder. Security camera footage shows Ramy entering the building earlier that night, but not going to Adam’s apartment. He is evasive in interviews and admits Adam had told him something about a money trail involving a person close to him.

Meanwhile, police search Adam’s apartment and discover a hidden encrypted folder on his computer. In it is a video recorded by Adam before his death. Adam looks directly into the camera and says:

“If you’re watching this, I’m probably dead.”

He explains that someone has been using his identity and moving money through his accounts. He says: “I know who is behind it.”

The investigation opens multiple avenues. Ramy has a key to a condemned storage room in the building. Inside, police find a collection of secret photographs of Adam taken in public places: the café, the street, outside his work, and outside his apartment. Someone had been watching him. The photos suggest an organized campaign of surveillance, not a random murder.

### Act III – The Brother, The Car, and the Clock

Security footage reveals a black car parked outside the building at 3:05 AM. It remains there for 19 minutes and then leaves. The car is registered to Ramy.

Ramy finally confronts Samer and confesses that he had been afraid for Adam because of Adam’s brother, Mazen. Police investigate and discover Mazen had returned to the city only two days before and had not informed anyone.

But Mazen is gone. He is missing. The investigation begins to form a new theory: Adam knew more than he was telling; someone close to him was in danger; and the murder scene may not be what it seems.

Then Samer and the team examine the wall clock in Adam’s apartment. It wasn’t broken—it had been moved by hand. Behind it is a tiny digital memory card. On the card is another video recorded by Adam.

Adam reveals the truth:

“I didn’t die at 3:17.”

The video shows Adam installing a hidden recording device behind the clock before leaving the apartment. This means the murder scene was staged and Adam was alive when Ramy left the apartment. Ramy had not killed Adam. He had been searching for evidence.

### Act IV – The Final Twist

The investigators realize that the body found in the apartment was not Adam’s. It was another unidentified man. Adam staged an apparent death to lure the real culprit into the apartment, where he would search for hidden files and evidence. The emergency call at 3:17 was part of the trap. It was not a panic call. It was a signal designed to push the real perpetrator into action.

The financial conspiracy is the heart of the case. Adam had uncovered that his identity had been used to move money. He had recognized the method and the player. The investigation now pivots from solving a murder to uncovering a coordinated criminal operation, one in which someone close to him had everything to gain.

Three days later, Detective Samer receives an anonymous envelope. Inside is a photograph of Adam, alive, in an unknown location. On the back is a message:

“NOW IT’S YOUR TURN TO START THE INVESTIGATION.”

The film ends on a black screen. A digital clock appears: 3:17. Cut to black.

## Tone and Visual Language

The film must feel grounded, dark, quiet, and realistic. It should focus on tension, not spectacle. The atmosphere is driven by:

- narrow Egyptian apartment corridors
- dim practical lighting and old fluorescent bulbs
- cold urban nights and wet streets
- realistic silence, distant ambience, and subtle mechanical sounds
- disciplined, serious police investigation
- slow, methodical discovery

The pacing is patient, with deliberate pauses before each major revelation. The suspense comes from what is left unsaid, what is missed, and how the visual clues keep pointing to a truth that is only gradually revealed.

## Themes

- Identity and surveillance
- Trust and betrayal
- Criminal manipulation through technology and money
- The line between victim and architect
- Hidden truth beneath the surface of a seemingly impossible crime

## Emotional Core

At the center of the film is Detective Samer’s quiet, disciplined investigation. He does not chase sensationalism; he follows the facts, every small clue, every contradiction. The film’s most powerful tension lies in his realization that a crime story he thought he understood is actually a deliberate trap built by the victim.

## Conclusion

3:17 is a stylish, grounded, slow-burn Egyptian crime thriller that uses realism, atmosphere, and layered storytelling to build a case that seems impossible at first and elegant in hindsight. The final reveal reframes the story entirely: this was not a murder mystery but a trap, a deception, and a battle between an investigator and a criminal system that had intentionally been built around Adam’s identity.
