# Screenplay Structure — 3:17

## Opening Sequence

### Scene 1 – The Emergency Call

- 3:17 AM.
- A mysterious call is placed to the police from Adam Mansour’s phone.
- The call begins with silence and heavy breathing.
- A metallic object falls.
- A whisper is heard through the static: “Don’t look behind you.”
- The line cuts.
- The call ends without any further speech.
- This is the inciting event and the film’s first major emotional shock.

### Scene 2 – The Arrival

- 3:31 AM.
- Detective Samer and officers arrive at an old apartment building.
- The building is dark, aging, filled with narrow corridors and old metal doors.
- The apartment door is locked from inside.
- The officers have no forced entry and no obvious signs of a break-in.

### Scene 3 – The Body

- Samer enters the apartment.
- The room is dim, lit only by the glow of a silent television.
- The coffee cup sits on the table, untouched.
- Adam is found dead on the floor.
- There are no obvious wounds, no fight, no sign of an intruder.
- Samer notices the old wall clock, frozen at 3:17.
- On the desk: a handwritten note reading: “I KNOW WHO DID IT.”

## Act I

### Scene 4 – The Impossible Crime

- Samer studies the apartment.
- He notes the locked door, the silence, the unchanged environment, and the impossible timeline.
- There are no signs of forced entry, no glass broken, no evidence of a struggle.
- The phone call from 3:17 suggests Adam was alive after the estimated time of death.

### Scene 5 – The Phone Investigation

- Adam’s phone is seized.
- The 11-second call is extracted and replayed.
- The audio contains only breathing, a metallic sound, and the whisper: “Don’t look behind you.”
- Samer realizes the sound could be anything: a dropped object, a key, a metal piece, a weapon, a clock component.

### Scene 6 – First Interview

- Ramy is interviewed as Adam’s close friend.
- He says Adam had called him earlier that evening and insisted they meet.
- Ramy says Adam had been acting strangely, claiming he had discovered something about a person close to him.
- Ramy denies having been in the apartment or having any motive.

## Act II

### Scene 7 – The Surveillance Photos

- Investigators discover a locked storage room in the building.
- Inside is a box containing photographs of Adam taken from multiple locations.
- Adam had been stalked over the past several weeks.
- The photos show him at a café, on the street, near his workplace, and outside his apartment.
- The surveillance suggests someone very close to Adam had access to his routine, or at least knew his daily movements.

### Scene 8 – The Hidden Folder

- Samer searches Adam’s computer.
- He discovers a hidden encrypted folder.
- Inside is a video recorded by Adam before his death.
- Adam looks into the camera and says:

“If you’re watching this, I’m probably dead.”

He continues:

“I’ve discovered someone is using my identity and moving money through my accounts. I know who’s behind it.”

### Scene 9 – A New Theory

- The investigation shifts from murder to financial conspiracy.
- Samer begins to realize Adam knew his life had been manipulated and that someone was exploiting him.
- The clue chain points to a network of identity theft, money laundering, and intimidation.

### Scene 10 – Ramy Under Pressure

- Security footage from the building shows Ramy entering the apartment complex earlier that night.
- However, the cameras do not show him entering Adam’s apartment itself.
- Samer later notices something important: when Ramy leaves the building, he is holding a key.
- That key is later found to open a now-abandoned storage room in the building.

## Act III

### Scene 11 – The Black Car

- Investigators review all nearby camera footage.
- At 3:05 AM, a black car is seen parked outside the building.
- It remains there for 19 minutes.
- The car belongs to Ramy.

### Scene 12 – Confrontation

- Samer meets Ramy and confronts him with the footage and the key.
- Ramy breaks under pressure.
- He admits he was afraid for Adam.
- He says Adam had told him he was in danger because of Adam’s brother, Mazen.

### Scene 13 – Mazen

- Police investigate Mazen.
- They learn he had returned to the city two days earlier and had kept it secret.
- No one knows where he is.
- Samer starts to suspect the case is deeper than a single suspect or single betrayal.

### Scene 14 – The Hidden Memory Card

- During the forensic inspection of the wall clock, Samer realizes it was not broken.
- Someone had deliberately moved its hands.
- Behind the clock is a tiny digital memory card.
- It contains a second video recorded by Adam.

### Scene 15 – The Truth in the Recording

Adam appears on the recording and says:

“I didn’t die at 3:17.”

The recording shows Adam installing a hidden device behind the clock before leaving the apartment. The device captured the room after he left.

The recording reveals that Ramy enters the apartment later and searches the desk for evidence. Ramy did not kill Adam. He was searching for the files. He was not the killer.

The scene reframes the entire story and redefines the relationship between suspect, witness, and victim.

## Act IV

### Scene 16 – The Reframe

- Samer realizes the body in the apartment was not Adam’s.
- The victim was another unidentified man.
- Adam had built a deliberate deception to set a trap.
- Adam was alive when Ramy left the apartment.

### Scene 17 – The Financial Conspiracy

- The investigation reveals that Adam had discovered someone using his identity and moving illicit money through his accounts.
- The conspiracy is deeply organized and involves manipulation of legal and financial systems.
- The hidden files, the watch, the apartment, and the timing were all designed to draw the culprit out.

### Scene 18 – The Envelope

- Three days later, Samer receives an anonymous envelope.
- Inside is a photograph of Adam alive, standing in an unknown location.
- On the back is the line:

“NOW IT’S YOUR TURN TO START THE INVESTIGATION.”

### Final Scene – Black Screen

- Fade to black.
- A digital clock appears: 3:17.
- Cut to black.

## Narrative Notes

This screenplay is built on contradiction and delayed revelation. It begins as a locked-room mystery, becomes a surveillance investigation, evolves into a financial conspiracy thriller, and ends as a trap story where the “victim” is the architect of the deception.

The key to the film is the disciplined buildup: each clue matters, and the final twist should feel inevitable once the audience is placed in the correct frame of reference.
