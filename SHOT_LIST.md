# Production Guide — 3:17

## Visual Style

### Cinematic Direction
The film should feel like a premium streaming crime thriller shot in a grounded, realistic, cinematic style. The photography should be restrained but sharp, with careful framing, practical lighting, and exacting attention to environment detail.

### Key Visual Language
- Slow tracking shots through hallways and apartment corridors
- Controlled handheld camera in moments of tension
- Dead-silent close-ups of eyes, hands, clocks, and evidence
- Overhead shots of the apartment layout and crime scene
- Low-angle perspective when entering key locations
- Long observational pauses before each reveal
- Security-camera framing and digital imagery as evidence rather than spectacle

### Camera Behavior
- Intimate, patient, observant framing
- Realistic human movement and natural pacing
- Deliberate push-ins during revelations
- No exaggerated action choreography
- Thoughtful negative space before and after important discoveries

## Lighting

### Core Lighting Approach
The film must be dark, atmospheric, and realistic. The city should feel alive in a quiet way, but the apartment should feel isolated and psychologically oppressive.

Key lighting sources:
- cold moonlight through windows
- dim yellow corridor lights
- old floor lamps and practical interior lighting
- computer screen glow
- television static glow
- small practical light sources used as clues or emotional markers

### Color and Exposure
- realistic night exposure with restrained contrast
- warm artificial light mixing with cool exterior light
- deep shadows, limited fill light
- subtle film grain for authenticity
- realistic reflections and textures on glass, metal, and wood

## Sound Design

### Core Atmosphere
The sound must be rich in realism but subtle in emotional impact. The film should rely more on silence and ambience than obvious score manipulation.

### Sound Elements
- distant traffic
- apartment-building ambience
- footsteps on old stairs
- door creaks
- clock ticking
- subtle breathing
- electrical hum
- television static
- police radio static
- far-off dogs barking
- metallic clinks or small objects dropping

### Audio Strategy
- Silence before key discoveries
- Low-frequency tension during investigation scenes
- Ambient city noise used to maintain realism
- Carefully placed room tone for apartment scenes
- Sound becomes vital in identifying small clues such as the metallic object in the emergency call

## Music

### Score Style
Minimal, dark, cinematic, and restrained.

### Instruments
- deep bass drones
- piano notes
- atmospheric strings
- sparse, restrained chord progression
- rising tension during investigative breakthroughs
- silence immediately before major twist reveals

### Musical Rule
The score should never overpower the realism. It should support the silence and the dread rather than announce emotion.

## Editing

### Pacing
- slow during investigation and exploration
- shorter, more clipped cuts for revelations and deductions
- repeated visual clues before meaning is revealed
- editorial rhythm that mirrors Samer’s thinking

### Reveal Strategy
- do not expose the final twist early
- use visual repetition of the clock, the key, the apartment layout, and the recorded audio to build depth
- tension should come from recognition, not obvious exposition

## Locations

### Apartment Building
- old Egyptian apartment complex
- narrow staircases
- dim corridors
- worn walls
- aging metal doors
- dusty windows
- realistic urban aging and wear

### Adam’s Apartment
- old living room
- wooden furniture
- old wall clock
- television
- desk with computer
- coffee cup on table
- scattered papers and light clutter
- isolated, intimate, slightly claustrophobic ambience

### Exterior Locations
- quiet street at night
- controlled urban realism
- distant traffic, ambient life, and soft city lights
- realistic Egyptian neighborhood textures and building forms

## Continuity Standards

To maintain narrative clarity and premium realism, the following must remain consistent:

- Samer’s exact suit, hair, and beard throughout the entire film
- Adam’s exact appearance across all flashbacks, videos, and present-day scenes
- Ramy’s exact build and clothing across all scenes
- Mazen’s exact visual identity whenever shown
- apartment layout, furniture placement, and the wall clock position
- the note, the phone call audio, and the recording devices
- all timeline markers: 3:17, 3:31, 3:05, and the final envelope timing

## Performance Direction

### Acting Style
- natural realism
- minimal melodrama
- grounded facial expressions
- subtle reaction acting
- very believable everyday language and body language

### Performance Rule
The film should never feel stylized or exaggerated. Realism is the emotional engine.

## Final Creative Summary

3:17 is designed as a premium Egyptian crime thriller with a grounded emotional core and a deeply layered mystery. It uses meticulously controlled visuals, silence, and structure to build tension. The film invites the audience to think alongside the detective while the investigation slowly reveals that the apparent murder was actually a trap carefully staged by the victim himself.
